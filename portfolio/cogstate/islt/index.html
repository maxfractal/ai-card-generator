Objective

The Cogstate International Shopping List Test (ISLT) is a word list learning game used in clinical trials to detect cognitive decline over time — and one of Cogstate’s core products. I was tasked with researching and updating both the UX and UI to better support raters administering the test and reduce data errors in the field.

  • Client: Cogstate
  • Role: Lead UX Designer
  • Scope: Research, UX strategy, interaction design, UI modernization, usability testing

Problem

A 2016 tablet version of ISLT was generating severe data errors in several clinical trials — not because of the science, but because of the interface.

Through user interviews and customer support logs, I traced many of the issues back to the raters (clinicians) administering the game:

  • Small, dense UI elements led to frequent mis-taps and incorrect data entry.
  • Raters struggled to know which phase of the test they were in or what came next.
  • The overall experience felt “like software from the ’80s,” undermining trust and ease-of-use.

Goal

The primary goal was to rethink the UI so raters could no longer “accidentally” input the wrong data — while addressing broader pain points around navigation, onboarding, and session flow.

  • Reduce data errors during test administration.
  • Give raters clear context: where they are in the session and what remains.
  • Simplify onboarding and startup so raters don’t have to rely on outdated manuals.
  • Modernize the look and feel to better match Cogstate’s brand and current expectations.

User Research

I conducted a series of 30-minute interviews with 11 Rater Trainers and two internal support staff, all with at least one year of experience using the app. After synthesizing the interviews and reviewing support tickets, five primary pain points emerged:

  • Raters were often unsure which phase of the test they were in.
  • The experience felt disconnected between phases, and it was unclear how to proceed.
  • Recording responses was difficult for raters with “fat fingers” on dense tap targets.
  • Figures and findings were difficult to read during a live test session.
  • The overall look and feel felt dated — “like software from the 80s.”

Before: What it was

The original ISLT app was underperforming in clinical studies and generating avoidable data errors. The interface was dense and visually dated, with small tap targets, minimal guidance, and a disjointed flow that didn’t align with how raters worked during a visit.

Usability testing revealed that raters frequently leaned on old user manuals just to complete basic tasks in the app — a signal that the product’s mental model didn’t match their reality.

After: What I designed

I designed a cleaner, more modern interface that not only reduced incoming data errors but also gave clinicians clearer context: which stage they were in, how far they had to go, and options for pausing or adjusting mid-game.

The new design emphasized larger, more forgiving controls, guided phase transitions, and a more legible presentation of findings during live sessions.

What is the ISLT?

The Cogstate International Shopping List Test (ISLT) is a word list learning game used in clinical trials to measure cognitive decline over time.

Each session consists of 3–4 rounds. In each round, a clinician reads the same 12 nouns aloud, then the participant recalls as many words as they can. Both the reading and the recall make up a single round.

There is a 15-minute break between each round, with a final delayed recall around an hour later. The goal is to measure episodic memory across global clinical trials with standardized administration and cross-cultural appropriateness.

Wireflow: Login & Landing

I mapped the login and landing flows to make it easier for raters to see upcoming visits, access the correct subject, and launch directly into the appropriate test without hunting through nested menus.

Start Game — Round 1 Read Phase

The read phase needed to guide raters through reading each word clearly, pacing the session, and giving them control to pause or resume without losing context.

In-Game — Recall Phase

The recall phase focused on making it easy for raters to quickly capture verbal responses, correct mistakes, and see progress across the 12-word list without introducing new sources of error.

In the final in-game designs, we emphasized clarity of state (current round, remaining steps), visibility of key controls, and forgiving patterns for handling mis-taps or mis-heard responses.

Style Guide

The updated ISLT visual system aligned more closely with Cogstate’s brand while improving readability and reducing cognitive load for raters working long days in clinical settings.

Updated styling for ISLT within the broader Cogstate brand system.

Reflection

ISLT is a great example of how small UX decisions can have outsized impact on scientific outcomes. By centering raters’ real workflows — not just the ideal protocol — we reduced data errors, sped up training, and modernized one of Cogstate’s most important tools without compromising the integrity of the underlying assessment.
