Project Overview

Lila is a voice-enabled mobile game based on memorization. The project began with a deceptively simple brief: take one of Cogstate’s most successful games used in clinical trials and make a consumer version for smartphones.

The Challenge

The creative brief for Lila was beautifully simple: take the “International Shopping List Test” game—considered a gold standard for cognitive assessment in clinical trials—and commercialize it for a broad, non-academic audience.

In practice, this was more creatively challenging than it sounded. We needed to preserve the scientific rigor and sensitivity of the original test while designing a charming, approachable experience that everyday users would enjoy.

User Research

After an initial design sprint with Cogstate’s in-house scientists, I conducted interviews with five participants to ground the concepts in real-world behavior and expectations. These conversations surfaced key questions around perceived value, anxiety around “testing,” and the role of voice in mobile play.

Due to privacy sensitivities, I created proto-personas instead of fully identified personas. This kept users at the center of the process while protecting individual identities and clinical details.

Wireframes & Flows

I created low-fidelity wireframes to map out primary tasks and scenarios—from onboarding and environment checks to in-game moments and post-test feedback. These screens formed the basis of an early, tappable prototype for stakeholder reviews.

Device & Environment Checks

Because Lila relies on accurate voice capture, the device and environment checks were critical. Working closely with Cogstate’s science team, I designed flows that guide users through microphone setup, ambient sound checks, and readiness confirmation—without overwhelming them with clinical language.

Iterations & Stakeholder Feedback

Once stakeholders could interact with the prototype, feedback surfaced opportunities to refine both the UX and UI:

  • Clarifying language around “tests” to feel more like play than assessment.
  • Making microphone and environment checks feel lightweight, not punitive.
  • Introducing visual cues that reinforced success and progress.

I iterated on several details in the interface while gradually layering in branding and visual design, balancing warmth and approachability with the seriousness of cognitive assessment.

High-Fidelity Prototype

With the interaction model validated, I evolved the prototype into a fully branded experience. Typography, color, and motion reinforced a calm, supportive tone while still feeling modern and playful.
