Voice UX Mobile User Research Design Sprints Usability Testing

Objective

Create a consumer-facing mobile experience based on Cogstate’s “International Shopping List Test” — a validated cognitive assessment used in clinical trials — while preserving the underlying science and making it feel like a game, not an exam.

  • Client: Cogstate
  • Role: UX Designer — research, flows, wireframes, prototyping
  • Focus: Voice interaction, device readiness, anxiety-free test framing

Problem

How do you turn a serious cognitive assessment into something playful and emotionally safe — without losing its clinical value?

  • Existing experience and language were designed for clinicians, not players.
  • Voice capture quality (mic + environment) directly impacts data quality.
  • Users felt anxious about “being tested” and weren’t sure what would happen with their voice data.

Approach

  • Design Sprint with Scientists: Co-created flows with Cogstate’s in-house science team to anchor UX in clinical requirements.
  • User Research: Interviewed 5 participants to understand expectations around voice, privacy, and “test” framing.
  • Proto-Personas: Represented key behaviors and needs while respecting privacy constraints.
  • Wireframes & Wireflows: Mapped the journey from device check to in-game memorization and post-session feedback.
  • Iterative Prototyping: Used a clickable prototype to refine copy, structure, and visual tone with stakeholders.

Research & Early Flows

After the first design sprint, I moved into qualitative research with five participants. These sessions revealed a few critical themes:

  • “Test” language triggered anxiety — people didn’t want to feel evaluated.
  • Players needed reassurance about how their voice input was recorded and used.
  • Guidance around environment (quiet room, mic distance) was necessary but had to feel friendly.

Wireframes & Device Readiness

I created low-fidelity wireframes to explore how players would move from onboarding into the voice-driven test. The flow needed to establish trust, confirm device readiness, and clearly signal what would happen next.

Early wireframes representing the main scenarios and test flow.

Device & Environment Checks

Working with the science team, I designed a device check flow that guided players through microphone access and environment setup without feeling harsh or overly technical.

Wireflow showing system and device checks leading into the game.

Iterations & High-Fidelity Design

Once stakeholders could interact with a clickable prototype, feedback surfaced specific opportunities to refine both UX and UI:

  • Reframing “test” language so Lila felt more like a game session than an exam.
  • Softening device check copy and micro-interactions to feel supportive.
  • Clarifying progress states and feedback after each memorization attempt.

Visual Language

The visual system for Lila needed to feel calm and trustworthy, with enough personality to stand apart from a clinical app. Typography and color choices focused on clarity, warmth, and accessibility.

Typography system designed for readability and a reassuring tone.

Reflection

Lila sits at the intersection of gaming and clinical assessment. This work reinforced how much language, framing, and micro-interactions matter when people feel like they’re being evaluated. By designing the experience around emotional safety and clear guidance, we made it easier for players to engage with a serious assessment — and for Cogstate to maintain the integrity of their underlying science.
