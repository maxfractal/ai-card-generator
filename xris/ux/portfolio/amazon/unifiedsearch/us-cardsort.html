Unified Search | Card Sort Preliminary Findings

Background

In 2020, AWS launched Unified Search for the Console Experience. Now, for Re-Invent 2021, we are integrating additional content into the Unified Search experience, including documentation, tutorials, support articles, blogs, and more. In the past, AWS customers needed to leave the AWS console to navigate to other AWS websites such as AWS Documentation, AWS Getting Started, AWS Knowledge Center and AWS Marketplace to find the information they needed, but AWS customers expect to easily information while working in the AWS console. This information architecture (IA) study was designed to help us understand if the new Unified Search content categories resonate with existing and prospective AWS customers and if the category labels are helpful when looking for specific types of information.

Although we are still in the process of data collection as of 10/4, these preliminary findings were shared to support the cross-functional team prepare for Fit and Finish on 10/7. A research report will be shared with the team after quant/qual data analysis, ECD 10/15. Objectives • Understand if the search category naming conventions makes sense • Learn existing and prospective AWS customers’ mental models around which types of content they would expect to find within each search category • Determine if the marketing content meets customers’ expectations, especially the related pages? o Blogs, knowledge articles, etc. make up only ~10% of marketing pages whereas 90% falls into "related pages"

Approach

To address the study objectives, the researcher will administer a 30-minute remote unmoderated hybrid card sort with 39 participants across existing and prospective AWS customers. Participants included developers (n=10), IT pros (n=10), business decision makers (n=10), and IT decision makers (n=9). The developer and IT pro profile represented existing AWS customers, while the BDM and ITDM profile represented prospective AWS customers. We first asked participants to describe their expectations and previous experiences with search. From there, participants were presented with the list of categories and were asked to describe what information they expected to find under each term. Next, they transitioned to the hybrid card sorting activity that was the primary activity for the session. Finally, participants were shown an image of unified search and were asked to describe their initial reactions to the look and feel of the unified search experience.
